Causal Batch Whitening: after the leak fix

What changed once the training-mode leak was found and fixed, and what we tried since. The full feature list and the pre-fix experiments are in bw_paper_vs_code.html.

Summary.
  • Training-mode BW leaked each sequence's future tokens once two BW layers were stacked. The fix (bw_renorm) whitens the forward pass with the stored statistics and keeps the gradient through the batch statistics. It is causal by construction, and the leak is now checked by the unit test and at every training start.
  • nanoGPT: the fixed BW still beats LayerNorm (about 8% lower mean train loss), half the old, leak-inflated margin. Shared batch statistics (bw_renorm_stats=batch) halve its cost at no loss.
  • GPT-2: no setting beats LayerNorm over a longer run yet. The best (momentum 0.3, fix_factor 0.4, BW in layers 0–5 only) is 2–3% ahead early, 0.5% at step 2000 and level by step 4000 (train +0.2%, val tie), at 1.25× LayerNorm's time per iteration.

The leak and the fix

Leak. Leave-one-out statistics keep one BW layer causal, but with two layers there is a path through another sequence: layer k writes sequence b's future into sequence s's activations, and layer k+1 reads them back into b's statistics. On GPT-2 (B = 12) the model exploited it within 2,000 steps: training-forward loss 1.60 against 9.0 in eval mode. All nanoGPT numbers in the main report are eval-mode losses and are not inflated, but its training may have used the leak.

Fix: Batch Renormalization applied to whitening (bw_renorm=True).

  • Forward: whiten with the buffers from earlier steps, LR−1(x−μR), exactly what inference computes. No current-batch value enters the forward pass, so training and evaluation compute the same function.
  • Gradient: through the current-batch statistics, written as R LB−1(x−μB) + d with R = LR−1LB and d = LR−1(μB−μR) held constant. The value is bit-for-bit the stored-statistics whitening.
  • Shared statistics (bw_renorm_stats=batch): since the batch statistics only shape the gradient, they no longer need to be per-sequence leave-one-out. One mean and covariance per batch, applied as a matrix product: 2× faster on nanoGPT and 2.4× on GPT-2, same results.
  • Guards: test_batch_orthonorm.py stacks 1–3 layers and fails if a causal mode leaks or the known leak is not detected. train.py measures the leak on the real model at startup (GPT.causal_leak, wandb causal_leak) and refuses to train if it is nonzero, unless --allow_causal_leak=True.

Two other fixes were considered and not built: causal per-position statistics (statistics at position t from positions ≤ t only), and a reference sub-batch (a quarter of the sequences whitened with stored statistics, the rest with statistics from that quarter). Both keep current-batch statistics in the forward pass.

Training loss relative to LayerNorm, 200-iteration moving average, clipped to ±10%.

  • Momentum matched to gradient accumulation gives an early gain. Momentum 0.7 (about 18 sequences in the buffers) was 1–2% behind throughout. Momentum 0.1–0.3 is about 2.5–3% ahead over iterations 200–800 in every configuration.
  • Full decorrelation hurts on GPT-2; mild is best. fix_factor 0.9 falls behind after iteration 800 with or without warmup (+4% and +6% at step 2000). With 0.1–0.4 the early gain mostly survives.
  • The warmup handoff is not the main problem. With bw_warmup=diag the switch to full covariance at step 1000 causes a jump, but turning warmup off does not remove the fade after iteration 800 at fix_factor 0.9. It coincides with the end of the learning-rate ramp (2,000 steps), when stale statistics matter most.
  • Gradient boost 3 diverges (+17% over iterations 800–1400; stopped).
  • BW only in the first layers is best, and cheaper. The diagnostic shows layers 1–4 are by far the worst conditioned. With BW in layers 0–5 and LayerNorm in 6–11, momentum 0.1 is the first run that is still ahead at step 2000 (−0.4%), at 1.06 s per iteration (LayerNorm 0.85 s). BW in layers 0–3 only (0.99 s) and momentum 0.3 give the same −0.4% to −0.5%.
  • The gain does not last. Run to step 4000, the best setting goes from −2.4% at step 1000 to −0.5% at 2000, −0.1% at 3000 and +0.2% at 4000. BW speeds up early training; LayerNorm catches up once the learning rate is at its peak. The BW input scale keeps growing (input_rms 1.0 at step 2000, 2.2 at 4000).

Conclusions and next steps

  • Causality: fixed with renorm, guarded by the unit test and a startup check. Pre-fix GPT-2 results and the pre-fix nanoGPT margins are leak-affected; the tables here are all post-fix.
  • nanoGPT: causal BW is about 8% better than LayerNorm in mean train loss and twice as cheap with shared statistics.
  • GPT-2: causal BW helps early (2–3%) but is level with LayerNorm by step 4000. What mattered: momentum matched to gradient accumulation (0.1–0.3), mild decorrelation (fix_factor 0.1–0.4), no warmup, and BW only in the first 4–6 layers. What did not help: full decorrelation, gradient boost, BW warmup.
  • Next, if continuing: (1) update the BW buffers once per optimizer step from all micro-batches instead of on every micro-batch, which gives exact 120-sequence statistics with no staleness; (2) BW in the first layers early and a switch to LayerNorm later, since the gain is early convergence; (3) the 4-GPU setup, with a 4× larger batch per step. Each is one short run (about 1 GPU-hour).
